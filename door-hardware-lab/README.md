# Hinge & Key: door hardware for houses and light commercial buildings

A single-file, offline primer on door hardware, from the hinge to the hardware schedule. Open `index.html` in any current browser. It has no dependencies or network calls. Your place and theme are kept in the browser's localStorage, and the page still works if storage is blocked. Door closers have their own plate, [Sweep & Latch](../door-closer-lab/), which this one links to.

## Sections

1. **The opening.** A clickable elevation of a house interior door, a house entry or a commercial hollow metal door, with hinge and lock locations that follow the door's size; the six jobs of hardware; vocabulary; standard preps (bores, backsets, the ANSI strike at 40 5/16 in., 5-10-equal hinge locations); kinds of door.
2. **Handing.** LH, RH, LHR and RHR from the secure side, a randomly rotated practice drill, what's handed, and the house-supplier convention that flips the answer.
3. **Hinges & pivots.** A selector for count (one per 30 in.), height, open width to clear trim (C = W − 2(T − 1/4)), weight, bearings, material, NRP and BHMA A156.1 number, with a plan of the door at 180°; eight hinge types; corners, tips, knuckles and installation.
4. **Locks & latches.** A plan of the latch closing into the strike showing the bevel, the guard plunger and a credit-card attack, with the door-to-strike gap as a control; lock types and standards; grades; making a house door kick-resistant.
5. **Lock functions.** A simulator for nine functions with outside and inside levers, keys, buttons and thumbturns, and an egress verdict; a table of ANSI numbers, uses and misuses.
6. **Cylinders & keying.** A five-pin tumbler cylinder with a change key, master key and any other key, showing the pin stacks, binding chambers and how many bittings turn it; key system terms; cylinder types.
7. **Exit devices.** When panic or fire exit hardware is required (occupancy, occupant load, large electrical and refrigeration rooms, fire rating), swing direction and the main-door key-lock exception; device types; trim functions.
8. **Pairs of doors.** Five configurations drawn with their hardware: manual and automatic flush bolts, coordinators, astragals, removable mullions, vertical rods, house French doors.
9. **Closers, stops & plates.** Closers in brief, a stop chooser, holders, and a protection plate sizer with the NFPA 80 16 in. rule.
10. **Seals & thresholds.** An ADA threshold profile checker; perimeter seals, door bottoms, thresholds, smoke, fire and sound seals.
11. **Electrified hardware.** Fail-safe and fail-secure strikes, locks, EL exit devices, maglocks and delayed egress against credential, power, fire alarm and request-to-exit; the parts of an access-controlled door; smart locks.
12. **Codes & fire doors.** IBC egress, ADA, IRC and hurricane product approvals; an ADA hardware check; a clear-width calculator; NFPA 80 fire doors, door ratings by wall, and the annual inspection checklist.
13. **Finishes & standards.** 21 BHMA finishes with US equivalents and base metals; matching a set; the ANSI/BHMA A156 series.
14. **Hardware sets.** Typical sets for thirteen openings, adjusted for fire rating and access control, with a copyable schedule text.
15. **Glossary.** About 85 searchable terms, each linked to its section.
16. **Check yourself.** 21 questions with explanations.

## Limits

These are educational models, simplified on purpose:
- Code references summarise the 2021 IBC and IRC, the 2010 ADA Standards, NFPA 80 and NEC 110.26 as commonly adopted. The edition your jurisdiction adopted, its amendments and the authority having jurisdiction govern.
- ANSI function numbers, grades and cycle counts are given as commonly published; sources and editions disagree on some (classroom security is F110 or F88 depending on the source). Check the standard or the maker's catalogue.
- The hinge selector, plate sizes, stop chooser and hardware sets are rules of thumb from common practice, not a specification. Door and frame makers' templates and fire labels govern.
- The latch and cylinder drawings are schematic; their dimensions are chosen to show the mechanism, not to match a product.

A self-test runs on load and logs to the console whether the calculators, the lock functions, the keying, the code checks, the hardware sets and every cross-reference check out.
