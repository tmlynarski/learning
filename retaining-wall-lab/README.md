# Batter & Drain: retaining walls for Northeast sites

A single-file, offline primer on retaining walls for houses and light commercial sites in New England, New York and New Jersey, from the leveling pad to the cap. Open `index.html` in any current browser. It has no dependencies or network calls. Your place and theme are kept in the browser's localStorage, and the page still works if storage is blocked.

## Sections

1. **The wall.** A clickable section through a 6 ft geogrid-reinforced block wall in glacial till: cap, units, batter, drainage stone, drain pipe, filter fabric, geogrid, reinforced zone, retained soil, leveling pad, embedment, foundation, cap soil and swale, toe. Vocabulary (exposed and total height, unbalanced fill, surcharge, toe and heel), the four ways to hold soil, and why the Northeast is different.
2. **How walls fail.** One wall animated through overturning, sliding, bearing, global (deep-seated) slip, internal bulging, water pressure and frost heave, with what you'd see, why, and how to prevent it.
3. **Soil & water pressure.** Rankine pressure on a vertical wall with slope, surcharge, water table and at-rest options, drawn as stacked soil, surcharge and water diagrams with a pressure–height chart; a table of the region's soils with unit weight, friction angle, the IBC Table 1610.1 and 1806.2 values, frost susceptibility and drainage.
4. **Stability check.** Gravity block, geogrid block, boulder or granite, and poured concrete cantilever walls, each with its own geometry (block depth and batter, grid length, base and top width, footing, toe, stem, frost depth, shear key), checked for overturning, sliding, bearing and the middle third, with the loads drawn on the section, the working shown, and a weights-and-arms table. Coulomb with wall friction for block and stone walls (as the NCMA method does); Rankine for the cantilever. It also reports how tall a plain block wall can stand in the chosen conditions.
5. **Wall types.** Eleven systems, from dry-laid fieldstone and two-, three- and four-man boulders to large-format blocks, gabions and anchored walls, with a chooser for height, room behind, machine access, look and budget.
6. **Block walls & geogrid.** The build in seven steps on the same section, a geogrid layout planner (courses, layer elevations, length, area) that hands its wall to the stability check, and notes on units, curves, corners and common mistakes.
7. **Concrete walls.** First-trial cantilever proportions drawn to scale with the reinforcing, checked and sent to the stability check; concrete for freeze-thaw exposure, cover, joints, drainage, reinforced CMU, footings on ledge, veneer.
8. **Drainage & frost.** Turn stone, pipe, outlets, fabric, cap and a misplaced downspout on and off and see how high water stands, how much it adds to the push, and whether the backfill grows ice lenses; frost depths by state; rigid versus flexible walls; salt; pipe details; perched water on ledge and till.
9. **Tiers, slopes & loads.** Two terraced walls with the lower wall's active wedge and the 2H spacing rule, and how much the upper one adds to the lower; common surcharges; slopes above and below, trees and utilities.
10. **Building & estimating.** The order of work from survey and 811 to the spring check, a material estimator for straight block walls (units, caps, stone, pipe, fabric, grid, trench), a checklist for before it's buried, compaction, and local stone names.
11. **Codes & permits.** A checker for permit, engineer, guard, property line, wetlands, nearby structures and commercial sites, and the IRC, IBC, ACI, ASTM, NCMA and OSHA rules behind it.
12. **Troubleshooting.** Twelve symptoms with likely causes, what to check and how to fix them.
13. **Glossary.** About 80 searchable terms, each linked to its section.
14. **Check yourself.** 22 questions with explanations.

## Limits

These are educational models, simplified on purpose:
- The stability check covers external stability only. It skips internal stability of geogrid walls, compound and global stability, settlement, seismic loads and the structural design of concrete stems and footings. Cohesion is ignored, uplift assumes a drained toe, and passive resistance is counted only when you ask for it (or across a shear key).
- Soil values are typical presumptive numbers chosen to sit near the IBC tables; a geotechnical report replaces them. Wall friction is taken as two thirds of the friction angle.
- The geogrid planner, cantilever proportions, wall chooser, drainage model and estimator are rules of thumb for planning and budgeting, not designs. Walls over 4 ft, or carrying a surcharge, need a licensed engineer and the unit maker's engineered tables.
- Code references summarise the 2021 IRC and IBC, ACI 318 and OSHA 1926 Subpart P as commonly adopted. Frost depths and wetland buffers are typical values; each state's amendments, the town and the conservation commission govern.

A self-test runs on load and logs to the console whether the earth pressure formulas, the stability checks, the gravity height limits, the geogrid planner, the chooser, the estimator, the permit rules, the tier and drainage models, every drawing and every cross-reference check out.
