# Mud & Tape: finishing gypsum board

A single-file, offline primer on finishing gypsum wallboard (GWB, drywall), from the bag of compound to the paint. Open `index.html` in any current browser. It has no dependencies or network calls. Your place and theme are kept in the browser's localStorage, and the page still works if storage is blocked.

## Sections

1. **The board.** A clickable section through a tapered joint and a butt joint (face paper, core, back paper, taper, wrapped edge, screw dimple, framing). Fourteen board types with thickness, weight, uses and finishing notes. Hanging rules, and a wall layout tool: change the wall, sheet length, orientation and stud spacing and see sheets, butt joints, tape footage and warnings (butt joints off a stud, joints at a door corner, slivers).
2. **Compounds.** Setting-type ("hot mud", the 5/15/20/45/90/210-minute bags) versus drying-type ready-mix (taping, all-purpose, lightweight, topping): what the number on the bag means, the chemistry of each, a side-by-side table, a job-by-mud matrix, mixing and handling, and dust, asbestos and lead. **The clock** models working time, recoat, sand and paint times against room temperature, humidity, coat thickness, mix water and airflow, with a microscope view (gypsum needles growing vs water leaving) and a fill that shrinks and cracks.
3. **Tape, bead & tools.** Paper, mesh, paperless and flexible tape; paper-faced, nail-on, vinyl, bullnose, L/J bead and control joints; knives, pans, banjos, boxes, sanders; paper vs mesh; how to bed tape.
4. **Coat by coat.** Step through a tapered seam, a butt joint, an inside corner, an outside corner and a fastener from hanging to paint, with the layers drawn in section and each coat's width measured.
5. **Levels of finish.** GA-214 Levels 0–5 on a drawn wall, a side-by-side table, a "which level do I need" helper and common misunderstandings.
6. **Light & sheen.** A raking-light simulator: an 8 × 4 ft patch with a tapered seam, a butt joint and screws, rendered for any level, primer, paint sheen and light angle and direction. Shows why Level 5 exists and what primer does.
7. **Primer & paint.** Primer types (PVA, acrylic, high-build, shellac, oil, waterborne stain blockers, penetrating sealer), the sheen ladder, painting new drywall step by step, and a paint quantity calculator.
8. **Textures.** Nine simulated textures (smooth, orange peel, knockdown, skip trowel, Spanish lace, popcorn, stomp, swirl, sand) with how each is applied, the finish level beneath, and how to patch it.
9. **Repairs.** A hole-size slider that picks the method (fill, mesh patch, California patch, backing and new piece), and thirteen repairs step by step: nail pops, cracks, door-corner cracks, tape blisters, torn paper, dings, medium and large holes, corner bead, water damage, sagging ceilings, over-cut boxes and inside corners.
10. **Defects & causes.** Sixteen common defects, what they look like, why they happen and how to prevent or fix them.
11. **Estimating.** Sheets, screws, tape, ready-mix, setting compound, corner bead and finishing days for a room.
12. **Glossary.** About 80 searchable terms, each linked to its section.
13. **Check yourself.** 16 questions with explanations.

## Limits

These are educational models, simplified on purpose:
- Set and dry times in the clock are illustrative: real set times spread widely around the number on the bag, and drying depends on airflow, board absorption and coat shape.
- Shrinkage percentages and the hardness, sanding and bond scores are relative values for comparison, not test data.
- The light simulator exaggerates slopes so features of a few thousandths of an inch show at screen size.
- Estimating and paint coverage use rules of thumb; manufacturers' coverage figures and real takeoffs govern.

Levels of finish are paraphrased from the Gypsum Association's GA-214; application follows GA-216 and ASTM C840; materials are covered by ASTM C1396 (board) and C475 (compound and tape). Fire-rated assemblies must follow their tested design. Product names such as Sheetrock and Durabond are used only as the common shorthand for a type of product.

A self-test runs on load and logs to the console whether the core calculations and every cross-reference resolve.
